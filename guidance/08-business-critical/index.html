NHSBSA defines Business Critical analysis as follows:

“Business Critical Analysis informs major policy, funding, statutory, regulatory or customer-impacting decisions. The analysis is essential to the achievement of business plan actions and priorities. Errors could lead to significant patient or user impacts or serious financial, legal, reputational damages or penalties”

The Analytical Assurance Decision Tool spreadsheet decides whether work is Business Critical. It is if any one of its six impact dimensions scores Level 4. Opening it needs an NHSBSA account with access to the Analytical Quality and Assurance site. Business Critical work is assured at Level 4 BCA and goes on the Business Critical register. Start at step 1.

Step 1. Decide at scoping whether it is Business Critical

Do this when the Scope is complete, before the Specification is written.

  1. Score the six impact dimensions with your commissioner in the Analytical Assurance Decision Tool spreadsheet, which describes each dimension at each level. Score each from Level 1 to Level 4 and take the highest. Opening it needs an NHSBSA account with access to the Analytical Quality and Assurance site.
  2. The work is Business Critical if any one dimension scores Level 4.
  3. Keep Level 4 for a material consequence. The tool says: “Publication status, Board consideration, Official Statistics designation, media interest, national visibility, FOI disclosure, ministerial interest or parliamentary scrutiny do not by themselves justify a Level 4 score.” Score those under public visibility and reach.
  4. If you and your commissioner disagree, escalate to the Analytical Quality and Assurance Lead.
  5. Save the completed Analytical Assurance Decision Tool and append it to the quality assurance plan, as its template asks. Record the answer, yes or no.
  6. Check whether the Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard applies to any algorithmic tool in the work. It does if the tool:
    • has a significant influence on a decision-making process with public effect, or
    • interacts directly with the general public.
    Record the answer either way. Algorithmic transparency and AI has the test.

Step 2. Name who is accountable

The commissioner names an Analysis Senior Responsible Officer at scoping, with the Analytical Quality and Assurance Lead.

  • They must be senior enough to carry the risks across the lifecycle.
  • They approve the work as fit for purpose before it is used.
  • The commissioner may take the role.

More detail: the Analysis Senior Responsible Officer role.

Step 3. Read the assurance level

Read your level in the Analytical Assurance Decision Tool you used in step 1. The Analytical Quality and Assurance Lead confirms a Level 4 BCA level.

Text description of this diagram

A grid of sixteen cells. Impact of error, the highest single score, runs up the side from 1 at the bottom to 4 at the top. Likelihood of error, the highest single score, runs along the bottom from 1 low to 4 very high.

  • Impact 4. Level 4 BCA at every likelihood. The row is outlined.
  • Impact 3. Level 1 Standard at likelihood 1, Level 2 Enhanced at 2, and Level 3 Comprehensive at 3 and 4.
  • Impact 2. Level 1 Standard at likelihood 1, Level 2 Enhanced at 2 and 3, and Level 3 Comprehensive at 4.
  • Impact 1. Level 1 Standard at likelihood 1 to 3, and Level 2 Enhanced at 4.

Two notes sit beside the grid. Business Critical: any impact dimension scored 4 gives Level 4 BCA, whatever the likelihood. Read your level where impact meets likelihood: complexity is one likelihood dimension, and it does not set the level on its own.

Note: a full check of the likelihood in the Analytical Assurance Decision Tool is not required. An impact dimension has already scored Level 4, so the work is Business Critical. In the impact 4 row of the matrix, every likelihood from Level 1 to Level 4 gives Level 4 BCA.

Write Level 4 BCA into the Specification and go on to step 4.

The Analytical Risk Assessment and Quality Assurance Framework may escalate work to Business Critical analysis. If it does, and the work was not designated Business Critical, score the six impact dimensions again with your commissioner. The work is Business Critical only if a dimension then scores Level 4.

Step 4. Get it on the register and arrange external assurance

Log the work with the Data Science, Statistics and AI team, which holds the Business Critical register. Do this whether or not the work is a model. The entry is added to NHSBSA’s public register of Business Critical analyses. NHSBSA reports on that work each year to the Data, Analytics and AI Governance Council, to the Department of Health and Social Care and in its annual report. See who to ask for how to reach the team.

At the same time, arrange an independent external assurer for the additional scrutiny and testing that Level 4 BCA asks for.

Add each part of the entry when it exists:

  • at scoping, the Analysis Senior Responsible Officer as the accountable officer, and the intended use
  • as they are produced, the user documentation and the guidance on quality assurance
  • at sign-off, the officer’s confirmation that the work is suitable for its intended use

Step 5. Do the work to Level 4 BCA

Do everything Level 3 Comprehensive requires. Follow the Level 3 checks in what your level requires.

Level 4 BCA adds these checks:

  • formal audits
  • external review
  • additional scrutiny of the documentation
  • scrutiny of the structural and conceptual assumptions
  • additional scenario testing

Check that everyone using and assuring the work has the skills and knowledge for it. Where they do not, arrange specialist induction or training.

The documentation stack

Complete the eleven documents below, generally in the order shown. Update an earlier document when a review shows it needs changing. For an analysis that already exists, review what is there, find the gaps and fill them first.

The picture shows who owns each document and the phase it belongs to. The table links each template. Opening them needs an NHSBSA account with access to the Analytical Quality and Assurance site.

Text description of this diagram

A grid with a lane for each role and a column for each of four phases: engagement and scoping, design, analysis, and delivery, communication and sign-off. Each lane is a pale block with the role’s name, and a rule separates one lane from the next.

The commissioner lane holds one document, in engagement and scoping: 3, the Analytical Assurance Decision Tool, which the commissioner completes with the analyst. The analyst lane holds eight. In engagement and scoping: 1, the analytical plan, or the team’s own version, and 2, the scope. In design: 4, the specification, 5, the quality assurance plan, and 6, the quality assurance appraisal, basic information. In analysis: 7, the analysis or model itself, 8, the technical guide, and 9, the user guide. In delivery, communication and sign-off the lane for the critical friend, as Lead Assurer, holds 10, the quality assurance appraisal, assurance review. An arrow goes down from it to 11, the quality assurance sign-off, which is drawn across the lane of the critical friend and the lane of the Analysis Senior Responsible Officer below it, because the critical friend signs off first and then the officer.

Three more templates support the stack: document control and information governance, knowledge management and version control. All three are in the Analytical Quality Assurance Templates folder. The risk and issue log and the assumptions register are part of the quality assurance appraisal.

  • Give each action in the quality assurance plan and the assurance review a priority of Must, Should or Could.
  • Hold the documentation centrally with the Analytical Quality and Assurance function, and send it each new production version of a model.
  • Name each file with the document type first, for example User_Guide_AnalysisName.

What each document holds, and how the stack is scored and held is in Resources.

Step 6. Say what is uncertain

Document the uncertainty. Quantify it wherever the method allows, as at Level 3.

Write down any assumption that would change the answer if it were wrong.

More detail: what uncertainty is, how to manage it and how to write it up.

Step 7. Get sign-off, then publish what was checked

Get sign-off from the Lead Assurer, then from the Analysis Senior Responsible Officer. Your commissioner then signs it off for release. Update the Business Critical register entry when the work is signed off.

Then publish the quality assurance statement with the output. From Level 3, it carries what Phase 4 lists. At Level 4 BCA it also carries:

  • the Business Critical register reference
  • the Lead Assurer, named, from outside the delivering team
  • the Analysis Senior Responsible Officer who approved the work as fit for its intended use
  • the quality assurance sign-off reference from the quality assurance appraisal

Step 8. Set a review date and approve any new use

The Analysis Senior Responsible Officer sets a review date with the commissioner, using the review schedule for your level in Phase 5, review.

Get the Analysis Senior Responsible Officer to approve any new use before it goes ahead. A new use is a different purpose, or a change in the context of its use.

Before any reuse, run the four checks before reuse. If any answer is yes, score the assurance level again.

Step 9. Review the work and revalidate the designation

Revalidate the designation as part of the Phase 5 review. The Analysis Senior Responsible Officer does this annually, and before any reuse for a new purpose.

Check whether work that was Business Critical still is, and whether work that was not has become Business Critical as its use widened.

After sign-off, keep assuring the work:

  • Check that it is used as intended, with its caveats.
  • Log any errors and record any improvements.
  • Keep data and assumptions up to date through scheduled reviews.
  • After any material change, review the scope, the specification, the user and technical guides, and the quality assurance appraisal and plan, in proportion to the change.
